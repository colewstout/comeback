One More Time

Automatic customer win-back for local businesses that don't have a marketing team. "One More Time" is the working name. Before spending money on branding, search the US trademark database for it (classes 9, 35 and 42) and pick a domain such as onemoretime.app.

Summary

Local service businesses lose regular customers quietly. Nobody notices that a client who came every five weeks hasn't been in for three months. Large businesses use CRM software to catch this. Small shops either don't have the tools, or they have a basic feature inside their POS that nobody set up.

One More Time takes a business's sales history, learns how often each customer usually buys, and flags anyone who is overdue. It then sends a three-step win-back sequence (check-in, discount, last call) by text or email, and reports each week how much money came back.

We charge $49–199 a month. The pitch is simple: one returning customer pays for the month.

Customer and niche

Your research made one thing clear: a general "win-back for everyone" tool gets squeezed by Square's free feature and by new AI startups. So we launch narrow and expand later.

Launch niche: appointment-based personal services

Start with hair salons, barbers and dog groomers. They have:

  • A predictable rebooking rhythm (4–8 weeks), so "overdue" is easy to measure and easy to explain.
  • A big, documented problem: only about 35% of first-time salon clients come back.
  • Owners who do everything themselves and won't set up marketing software.
  • No patient health data, so no HIPAA issues. Med spas and dental come later, with legal advice.

Pick whichever of the three your network reaches most easily, and make the website and messages speak only to that niche for the first 20 customers. The demo already supports switching wording by industry.

Ideal first customer

  • Owner-operated, 1–3 locations, 2–15 staff.
  • 500–3,000 past customers in their POS or booking app.
  • Not already paying for Podium, Birdeye or an agency.
  • Can export a customer or sales list to a spreadsheet (almost all can).

Expansion order

  1. Salons, barbers, groomers (months 1–6)
  2. Car detailers, fitness and yoga studios, nail and lash studios (months 6–12)
  3. Cafés, restaurants and retail with loyalty lists (year 2)
  4. Med spas, dental, chiropractic, after a HIPAA review (year 2+)

Product

What the first paid version must do

  1. Import: CSV upload from Square, Vagaro, Fresha, Booksy or a spreadsheet. One row per purchase: name, phone or email, date, amount.
  2. Rhythm: each customer's usual gap is the median time between their visits. First-time customers use the industry default. A customer is "due" at 1× their gap, "overdue" at 1.5× (adjustable), and "lapsed" at 3×.
  3. Sequence: Day 0 friendly check-in with no discount. Day 7 personal discount code. Day 21 last call. It stops the moment the customer comes back or replies STOP.
  4. Sending: text through Twilio and email through Resend or Postmark, inside quiet hours, at most one message a week per customer.
  5. Attribution: when the next upload shows a visit from a contacted customer, count it as recovered revenue. Discount code redemptions confirm it.
  6. Monday report: an email to the owner: "9 clients came back, $1,140 recovered."
  7. Billing: Stripe subscriptions with a 30-day free trial.

Items 1–3 and the report already exist as a working front-end in the website demo. That's your sales tool today.

Later, once people are paying

  • Direct Square sync through its API, so there are no more uploads. This is the biggest retention feature.
  • Booking app syncs (Vagaro, Fresha, Booksy, Mindbody) in the order your customers ask for them.
  • AI-written messages in the owner's voice, and two-way text replies routed to the owner's phone.
  • "First visit to second visit" sequence for new customers (the 35% problem).
  • Birthday and anniversary messages, review requests after a return visit.

Getting customers

Customers 1–10: your network, by hand

  • List every salon, barber, groomer or studio owner your parents, relatives and friends know. Ask for warm introductions, not cold calls.
  • Offer a free "lost customer audit": they send a sales export, you run it through the demo and show them how many customers are overdue and how much money that is. This takes you 15 minutes and is the best sales tool you have.
  • Run the first win-backs by hand if you need to (copy the messages from the demo and send them from a Google Voice number or their own phone). Proving results matters more than automation at this stage.

Customers 10–50: local and repeatable

  • Walk-ins: visit salons on Tuesday and Wednesday mornings (their slowest time) with a one-page printout of the audit offer.
  • Referrals: one free month for both owners when a customer refers another business. Salon and groomer owners know each other.
  • Facebook groups for salon owners, groomers and local business owners. Post case studies with real numbers, not ads.
  • Suppliers and educators: beauty supply reps, groomer schools and barber academies talk to hundreds of owners. Offer them a referral fee.

50+: scale channels

  • Square App Marketplace listing once the Square sync exists.
  • Short videos showing the Monday report ("this groomer got $1,900 back last month").
  • Partnerships with booking-software resellers and small marketing agencies (white label).

Sales scripts

Warm intro text
Hi [Name], I'm [your name], [mutual contact]'s [son/friend]. I built a tool that finds salon clients who've stopped coming in and brings them back with a text. Could I run a free check on your client list? It takes 15 minutes, and I'll show you how much money is sitting in clients who've gone quiet. No cost and nothing to install.
Discovery call questions (don't pitch until the end)
  1. About how many clients do you see in a month? How many are regulars?
  2. When a regular stops coming in, how do you find out?
  3. Do you do anything today to bring them back? How often does that actually happen?
  4. What's an average visit worth?
  5. What do you use for booking and payments? Could you export your client list?
  6. Have you tried marketing tools before? What happened?
The close, after showing the audit
You have [X] clients who are overdue, worth about [$Y] a month. If we bring back just 1 in 10, that's [$Z] a month, and the plan is $99. Founding members get setup done for them and half off for three months. Want me to turn it on this week? You approve every message before it goes out.
Common objections
  • "Square already does this." It has a basic version. Have you turned it on? (Usually no.) We set it up for you, learn each client's personal schedule, and show you the dollars every Monday.
  • "I don't want to spam my clients." At most three friendly messages over three weeks, and it stops the moment they book.
  • "I can't afford another subscription." Then start with Pay-as-you-recover: $29 a month plus 10% of what comes back.

Data model, for when you build the backend

  • businesses: name, industry, discount %, overdue threshold, sending hours, plan.
  • customers: business, name, phone, email, text consent, email consent, opted out.
  • visits: customer, date, amount, source (upload or sync).
  • sequences: customer, started, current step, code, status (active, won, stopped).
  • messages: sequence, step, channel, body, sent at, delivery status.

A daily job recomputes every customer's rhythm, starts sequences for anyone newly overdue, sends due steps within quiet hours, and marks sequences "won" when a new visit appears.

Legal and compliance

Texting is the main legal risk in this business. Get it right from day one.

  • Consent (TCPA): only text customers who agreed to receive texts from the business. A promotional text without consent can mean fines of $500–$1,500 per message. Give customers without text consent email only.
  • Carrier registration: US carriers require business texting to be registered (toll-free verification or A2P 10DLC). Each client business generally needs its own registration. Plan setup time for it.
  • Opt-out: include "Reply STOP to opt out" and honor it immediately. Respect quiet hours (no texts before 8am or after 9pm local time).
  • Email (CAN-SPAM): include the business's physical address and an unsubscribe link.
  • Data: a simple Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, plus a data processing clause saying the business owns its customer data and you only use it to provide the service.
  • Health businesses: avoid med spas, dental and therapy until you get advice on HIPAA.

An hour with a small-business lawyer before your first paid customer is money well spent. This section is a checklist, not legal advice.

First 90 days

Weeks 1–2: validate

  • Choose the launch niche (salon, barber or groomer) based on who you can reach.
  • Book 10 conversations with owners using the discovery questions. Don't sell yet.
  • Run 5 free "lost customer audits" with the website demo.
  • Buy the domain, set up business email, and check the name is available.

Weeks 3–6: pilot by hand

  • Sign 3 pilot businesses on the founding offer, free for the first month.
  • Send their win-backs semi-manually using the demo's messages. Track every return in a spreadsheet.
  • Form the LLC and open a business bank account.
  • Start building the backend: login, CSV import, daily job, email sending.

Weeks 7–10: automate and charge

  • Add Twilio texting with opt-out handling, then Stripe billing.
  • Turn the pilots into paid customers. Collect written testimonials with real dollar numbers.
  • Ship the Monday report email.

Weeks 11–13: grow

  • Start the referral program. Do walk-ins at 20 local shops.
  • Publish 2 case studies on the website.
  • Goal: 10 paying businesses and $700+ MRR by day 90.

Metrics that matter

  • Recovered revenue per customer per month. This is the product. Aim for at least 10× the subscription price.
  • Win-back rate: share of contacted customers who return within 30 days. Expect 8–20%. Treat vendor claims of 25–30% as a best case.
  • Monthly churn: under 4%. Anyone below 3× ROI is a churn risk; call them.
  • Time to first win-back: under 14 days from signup.
  • Audit-to-paid conversion: aim for 30%+.
  • Opt-out rate: under 2% per message. Higher means the wording or frequency is wrong.
